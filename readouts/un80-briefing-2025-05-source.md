## Why the analytical frame differs from Beyond GDP

Beyond GDP was a genuine support/oppose consultation. UN80 at launch is **not**: every delegation on the record welcomes the initiative in principle — there is no blocking bloc. Coding delegations on "do you support UN80" would produce a near-uniform green field with no signal.

The live tension, per the silo-C dossier's "what to watch," is **who controls the reform** and **what must be protected from it**. So the readout is organised around two fault-line axes, not a support spectrum:

- **X — Sovereignty insistence:** how strongly a delegation insists the process stay intergovernmental / Member-State-led, routed through the Fifth Committee and ACABQ, with the Secretariat in a supporting role (`ms-led`) vs. willingness to grant the SG latitude as Chief Administrative Officer to move quickly on cuts and structure (`sg-latitude`). Middle = both endorsed process and welcomed SG action without foregrounding either.
- **Y (selectable):**
  - **Safeguard priority** — foregrounds protecting development mandates / vulnerable-country support / pillar balance against cuts (`safeguard`) vs. foregrounds depth and speed of cuts/consolidation (`cut-appetite`), or neither (`neutral`).
  - **Cut appetite** — same axis surfaced directly: how aggressively a delegation pushes for post reductions, agency mergers, relocation.

`silent` on any axis = no explicit position recorded in *this* meeting.

The SG's own framing (opening + closing reply) is the anchor, not a coded dot: he pre-empted the sovereignty worry ("this is not a process aiming at circumvent the Member States"), committed the Secretariat track to the ACABQ/Fifth Committee budget process, and distinguished efficiency from the liquidity crisis ("we won't solve a liquidity crisis by reforming the organization... countries paying full and on time").

## Consensus (stated by nearly all speakers)

1. **UN80 is welcome / timely.** No delegation opposed the initiative in principle.
2. **Preserve the three pillars in balance** — peace & security, development, human rights. Explicit from EU, Singapore/SSG, AOSIS, Nordic-Baltic, China, Japan, South Africa, and reaffirmed by the SG ("this is not a process based on... back to basics").
3. **The liquidity crisis is about arrears, not structure.** G77, Cuba, China, Norway, South Africa name late/withheld contributions; G77 and China point at the single largest contributor (">90% of arrears"). SG agrees explicitly.
4. **Efficiency must not become austerity.** CARICOM ("efficiency must not become austerity"), G77 ("avoiding austerity driven models"), Switzerland (false economies), Cuba (evaluate all costs of relocation).
5. **Process must be inclusive / transparent / member-driven.** Universal — even the most pro-cut voices (US, Australia) frame reform as needing Member-State engagement.

## Fault lines

- **F1 — Member-State control vs. SG latitude (the defining divide).** Cuba and Russia insist on the tightest intergovernmental control (ACABQ, Fifth Committee, JIU; Cuba: no mandate change "if there is no guidance issued from legislative bodies"; Russia: rejects a "fait accompli"). The US sits at the opposite pole — it wants the SG, "uniquely positioned as the chief administrative officer," to drive deep cuts. Most blocs sit in between, endorsing process *and* urging speed.
- **F2 — Cut depth.** US is the clear maximalist ("The most significant way to achieve savings is to cut posts"; scrutinise compensation; merge agencies). Australia and Romania push bold structural change. Against them, the safeguard cluster (G77, AOSIS, LDCs, Africa Group, LLDCs, CARICOM) warns cuts must not hit development mandates, vulnerable-country support, or institutional knowledge.
- **F3 — Relocation / duty stations.** SIDS-sensitive: AOSIS notes "many SIDS are not present in UN offices that are not New York or Geneva," so relocation ≠ efficiency for them. Host states Austria (Vienna) and Switzerland (Geneva clusters) defend their hubs; Australia insists "we can't be New York centric" and Southeast Asian hubs be considered. SG confirms delocalisation is live (UNICEF, ITC, UNHCR → Nairobi) but "case by case."
- **F4 — Mandate ownership.** Repeated line: the SG reviews mandate *implementation*, but only Member States change mandates themselves. G77, Cuba, Japan, and the SG all draw this boundary explicitly ("We will not review the mandates themselves. Those are yours to decide on").

## Outliers to monitor into the negotiation

- **United States** — the only delegation foregrounding post cuts and compensation review as *the* mechanism, and the only one explicitly framing reform as rescuing the UN from those "sabotaging" it. Its position is the mirror image of the arrears critique that G77/China/Cuba level at "the only beneficiary of the maximum ceiling."
- **Russia** — supports the initiative but reframes it as a Member-State-owned exercise (invokes the 1985 "Group of 18" precedent), and asks whether Bretton Woods institutions are in scope (SG: no).
- **Host-state bloc (Austria, Switzerland)** — a cross-cutting interest orthogonal to the sovereignty axis: defending Vienna/Geneva against relocation, independent of North–South alignment.
