## Why the analytical frame differs from round 1 (frame note)

Round 1 (12 May 2025) was the SG **launching** UN80 to the plenary. The live tension was *who controls the reform* (Member-State control ↔ SG latitude) and *what must be protected*. That X-axis was chosen because nobody opposed UN80 itself.

Round 2 is the **first working session of the Working Group** the launch created — and it is procedural. The co-chairs (New Zealand + Jamaica) posed three guiding questions, all about **method**: (1) how to make discussions interactive/inclusive, (2) how to organise and sequence the work, (3) how to address the SG's Mandate Implementation Review report within the broader UN80 process. Delegations answered almost entirely on **process, not substance** — no delegation opposed the exercise, and the SG (via USG Guy Ryder) is now a *support* actor, not the driver. So the round-1 sovereignty X-axis no longer discriminates: the Secretariat isn't contesting anything.

The frame therefore rotates to the two live procedural tensions of a kickoff:

- **X — Process ambition / pace:** how a delegation positions on the co-chairs' fast timeline (Discovery Phase → 15 Dec 2025 progress report → outcome by March 2026). `pace-caution` = leads with *slow down / flexible, deconflicted schedule / defer substantive negotiation to 2026*, driven by the burden on small delegations during Committee season. `pace-drive` = leads with *ambition, quick wins, get concrete early*. `mid` = endorses ambition **and** flags the scheduling constraint without foregrounding either.
- **Y (selectable):**
  - **Scope breadth** — how wide a delegation wants the Working Group's remit. `broad` = extend beyond GA mandates to ECOSOC / Security Council / HRC and/or link Workstream 3. `narrow` = keep it to Workstream 2 / the SG report / GA mandates, don't import other work streams. `mid` = no explicit scope-widening or -narrowing.
  - **Report anchoring** — how tightly work should hew to the SG's report as the starting point. `anchor` = report is the foundation, structure work around its three-stage lifecycle. `beyond` = actively push for member ideas beyond the report, or (Russia) contest the report's internal sorting of "SG-prerogative" vs "for-Member-States." `mid` = report is useful input, neither foregrounded nor contested.

`silent` on any axis = no explicit position recorded in *this* meeting.

The co-chairs' and principals' framing is the anchor, not coded dots: co-chairs proposed a **Discovery Phase** of wide consultation reporting by **15 Dec 2025**, then a production phase to finalise by **March 2026**; PGA Baerbock called it a "make it or break it moment"; USG Guy Ryder set out the Secretariat's support role and the report's two tracks (actions the SG takes under his own authority; actions Member States may wish to consider).

## What the principals proposed (section 2 content — unique)

- **Co-Chairs (New Zealand / Jamaica):** a **Discovery Phase** — wide, inclusive consultation "sensitive to the competing demands on delegations' time" — to build a **package of principles and follow-up actions**; report back to the membership by **15 December 2025**; then a **production phase to finalise outcomes by March 2026**. Explicit scoping limits: **system-level only, "we will not be considering individual mandates"** (the key lesson from the ~20-year-ago review); focus on the **future**, not re-litigating the past. Welcomed ideas from outside the Working Group while "preserving the Member State driven nature."
- **PGA Annalena Baerbock:** framed the mandate review as part of UN80's three themes (efficiency / mandate review / system-as-a-whole); called this a **"make it or break it moment"**; UN80 is "not just an exercise in reform for reform itself... not a cost-cutting exercise for just cutting cost." Gave the "16 speakers in one hour" resolution example of embedded inefficiency; committed OPGA transparency support; asked delegations to brief **every head of state / minister** at High-Level Week on UN80.
- **USG Guy Ryder (Secretariat):** the SG report offers two tracks. **Internal (SG's own authority):** accelerate mandate registries, shorter/combined reports, new report formats, publish download statistics, strengthen oversight, apply Funding Compact commitments; a UN-system **roadmap** is being built and can be shared "if the Working Group requires it." **For Member States to consider:** reduce mandate-text length, frame mandates to allow SG flexibility, don't mandate new tasks without resources, prioritise requests, include **expiry/sunset clauses**, introduce collective reviews. Repeated: "**Member States... own the mandates**... we are here to be of assistance."

## Consensus (stated by nearly all speakers)

1. **The exercise is welcome / the tone is constructive.** No delegation opposed. Universal congratulations to the co-chairs.
2. **Member-State-driven, consensus-based, open/transparent/inclusive process.** Named by G77, SSG, African Group, EU, AOSIS, CARICOM, Algeria, GCC, Russia, and essentially all — this is the strongest consensus in the room.
3. **Learn from the failed ~20-years-ago mandate review.** G77, Singapore/SSG, Australia/CANZ, Liechtenstein, Brazil, Indonesia, India all cite it — the lesson is *do not review individual mandates one by one* (echoing the co-chairs).
4. **Preserve the three pillars in balance, protect the development pillar.** G77, African Group, AOSIS, CARICOM, PIF, Bangladesh, India, Switzerland, France, Norway, China — development-pillar protection is the safeguard line, sharpest from the Global South and SIDS.
5. **Schedule must accommodate small delegations / avoid clashing with the Committee season.** Near-universal among smaller missions (G77, AOSIS, CARICOM, PIF, El Salvador, Ecuador, Paraguay, Liechtenstein, GCC) — deconflict with main committees, early/predictable scheduling, circulate documents in advance.
6. **Member States are themselves part of the problem.** Argentina, Thailand, El Salvador, China, PGA: too many resolutions, reports, overlapping mandates — reform can't be aimed only at the Secretariat.
7. **The Secretariat's data/analysis support is welcomed** — especially the mandate registry (Switzerland, Pakistan, UK, Australia, India, Paraguay, Uruguay).

## Fault lines

- **F1 — Timeline ambition vs. flexibility (the defining divide of a kickoff).** The co-chairs set a fast track (Dec progress report, March 2026 outcome). Pushing to **honour/defend the pace**: the co-chairs themselves, PGA ("make it or break it"), Liechtenstein ("failure... cannot be an option"), the US ("ambitious set of principles"), UK, Thailand (frames the 6-month clock as a discipline). Pushing to **slow / flex the schedule**: G77 ("flexible timeline"), AOSIS (do substantive work "after the main session"), CARICOM, Pakistan ("substantive negotiations... should commence in the next year"), Brazil ("dedicating these initial months to listening"), African Group, Paraguay, Ecuador, El Salvador — driven by small-delegation burden during Committee season. Most sit in the middle: endorse ambition *and* flag the constraint.
- **F2 — Scope: GA-only vs. whole-system.** **Broad:** India ("all mandates emanating from General Assembly, Security Council, ECOSOC and Human Rights Council"; cover the entire lifecycle across all three work streams), Zambia (asks explicitly whether ECOSOC/SC mandates are in), Mexico (coherence "including mandates that go beyond the scope of the Secretariat"), Switzerland ("cover the entire United Nations system"). **Narrow / guard the boundary:** Pakistan (would **not** favour importing Workstream 3 proposals "unless the timeframe... is reconsidered"), Liechtenstein ("your mandate is Work Stream 2"), Zambia and others want the *relationship* to other streams clarified to avoid duplication. Co-chairs anchor the *practical* scope limit: **system-level, not individual mandates**.
- **F3 — Report anchoring vs. going beyond it.** **Anchor tightly:** Chile ("remain firmly guided by the report"), Brazil, Argentina and Mexico (organise work around the report's three-stage lifecycle — creation / delivery / review), Norway. **Go beyond:** African Group ("open and receptive to a broader array of ideas beyond those outlined in the SG's Report"), Algeria ("not solely from SG reports"), China ("ideas of their own"). **Contest the report's sorting (outlier):** **Russia** — the establishing resolution "is not differentiating the proposals," so recommendations the SG marked as *his own prerogative* also "deserve consideration by the Member States."
- **F4 — Sunset / expiry clauses.** A concrete substantive seam surfacing early. **For:** Thailand (pilot them on a trial basis), India (incorporate sunset clauses when creating/renewing mandates), Zimbabwe, the US (regrets sunset language "did not make it into the final text" of the revitalization resolution and hopes UN80 will "build on and strengthen" it). **Against / cautious:** **Argentina** ("preserve the General Rule that GA resolutions remain valid until fully implemented and not be undermined by sunset clause"); **Uruguay** (human rights and unresolved political issues "cannot simply have a sunset clause... no automatisms").

## Outliers to monitor into the negotiation

- **Russia** — the only delegation contesting the report's *internal sorting*: it argues the establishing resolution "is not differentiating the proposals," so recommendations the SG flagged as his own prerogative should **also** be open to Member-State consideration. A bid to widen intergovernmental control over the whole report.
- **Argentina vs. the sunset-clause camp** — the clearest substantive disagreement in an otherwise process-only meeting. Argentina wants to *preserve* the rule that GA resolutions stand until fully implemented (no sunset by default); Uruguay adds human rights / sensitive political files can't be time-limited. Against Thailand, India, Zimbabwe, the US who want sunset/expiry clauses piloted or built in.
- **India (+ Mexico, Switzerland) — maximal scope** — push to extend the remit to Security Council, ECOSOC and HRC mandates and the full lifecycle across all three work streams, against Pakistan/Liechtenstein who want the boundary held at Workstream 2.
- **Kuwait / translation failure** — the meeting had **no interpretation**; Kuwait (GCC) and Algeria both formally regretted the absence of multilingualism; Kuwait deferred its statement and delivered later in English. Procedural, but a live inclusivity grievance for the co-chairs.
